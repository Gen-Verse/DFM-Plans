# DFM Research Governance

DFM Community is designed to remain open while preserving scientific quality, attribution, and responsible interaction with real-world environments.

## 1. Evidence Over Claims

Novelty is not discovery. Model confidence, internal agreement, or an impressive demonstration is not sufficient evidence for a scientific claim.

Claims should be promoted according to the evidence they survive.

## 2. Attribution and Provenance

Research contributions should preserve who or what supplied consequential inputs, including:

- problem formulations
- domain insights
- data and environments
- algorithms and code
- experimental protocols
- human interventions
- validations and replications

The goal is to make important state transitions inspectable and attributable.

## 3. Independent Validation

Important claims should not rely only on the same system that proposed them to also select the evidence and judge the result.

Validation may include held-out execution, independent reviewers, alternative evaluators, replication, formal tools, simulation, or physical measurement depending on the claim.

## 4. Scoped Openness

DFM Community is open, but not every collaboration must be fully public.

Some datasets, commercial environments, wet-lab processes, safety-sensitive interfaces, or proprietary systems may require restricted access. Collaborations may therefore be open, partially open, or private.

Public claims should still clearly state the boundaries of available evidence and validation.

## 5. Research and Commercial Collaboration

Academic and commercial work can coexist in the DFM Community, provided that:

- conflicts of interest are disclosed where relevant,
- publication claims remain evidence-based,
- proprietary constraints are clearly separated from open scientific claims,
- and confidential information is not exposed without authorization.

## 6. Recursive and High-Consequence Systems

Updates that alter evaluators, long-term memory, permissions, experimental interfaces, or other high-radius components require stronger safeguards such as versioning, sandboxing, staged deployment, rollback, and independent evaluation.

> **Open participation does not imply unrestricted authority.**
